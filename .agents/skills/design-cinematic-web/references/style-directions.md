# Select an art direction

Choose from user goals, brand, content, audience, and available assets. The directions below are useful starting families, not an exhaustive list. Follow an explicit user direction unless it creates a concrete usability conflict; explain the localized adjustment. Avoid presenting a long style questionnaire when project context permits a good choice.

| Direction | Character and composition | Type/color/media | Motion | Appropriate adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spatial creative | Dominant project stage, layered media, quiet metadata, strong type contrast | Bold sans, contrasting canvas, selected luminous accent | Panels and measured scene transitions | Portfolio, creative campaign, entertainment; original reference |
| Luxury editorial | Generous negative space, asymmetric image/text, fine separators, large scenic imagery | Elegant serif display paired with readable body sans; ivory or pale canvas with colors from photography | Slow image expansion, restrained reveal, occasional relevant organic accents | Property, hospitality, fashion, premium service; video 087c… |
| Immersive technical | A coherent environment, geometric structures, numbered chapters, stable technical metadata | Extended/geometric sans display; strong brand hue with neutral reading surfaces | Apparent camera travel, chapter continuity, object-focused explanation | Technology/product storytelling; video b57d… |
| Travel narrative | Framing device, journey between scenes, hero subject retained across chapters | Clean sans, spacious composition, light/dark chapters and sky/media tones | Portal-like transition, subject reveal, map/globe stage if justified | Travel, transport, service experience; video 5770… |
| Restrained functional | Predictable grouping, clear navigation, content density matched to tasks | Readable type, semantic colors, tabular numbers where useful; neutral surfaces | Immediate or brief feedback; no decorative camera motion | Dashboard, checkout, booking, documentation, forms, public service |

Luxury does not require a serif; technical does not require red; travel does not require an airplane; cinematic does not require black. Transfer composition and pacing, not literal subject matter. A clinic may use a bright editorial treatment without floral framing; a software explainer may borrow portal framing around a product demo without imitating aviation imagery.

## Build a coherent family

Select one main direction. Add a supporting influence only for a concrete role, such as editorial typography plus a spatial product hero. Keep the same brand palette, spacing relationships, control shapes, and navigation language throughout. Vary composition by page purpose.

Separate independent axes:
- Mood: warm, precise, playful, formal, calm, energetic.
- Density: reading, discovery, comparison, workspace.
- Canvas: light, dark, mixed chapters, user-selectable if requested.
- Media: photography, illustration, product UI, rendered video, genuine interactive 3D, or typography-led.
- Motion: restrained, balanced, expressive, selected per region.

Do not infer low density from a premium brand or high motion from a technical product. A luxury booking form can be dense enough to complete quickly and remain visually refined.

## Define tokens for the chosen direction

Specify semantic tokens for canvas, surface, text, muted text, border, accent, focus, success, warning, and error. Separate decorative brand color from feedback colors. For example, a red technical brand still needs unmistakable error messages rather than using the whole red background as an error cue.

Specify display/body/data font roles, a responsive type scale, line lengths, spacing steps, container widths, corners, and layer order. Validate Vietnamese diacritics with actual headings and form labels. Avoid condensed display fonts for long reading. Choose values per project rather than copying a reference palette table blindly.

Define page-family variants for marketing, discovery, detail/reading, and workspace/transaction. Reuse component behavior but allow density differences. Do not force a large hero onto a login, checkout, settings, or article page.

## Asset feasibility

Make a short inventory of supplied, reusable, missing, and proposed assets. Identify whether a requested scene needs transparent imagery, multiple viewpoints, a 3D model, or a rendered sequence. Do not promise a convincing camera orbit from a single flat photograph. Prefer a strong 2D composition over a broken or misleading 3D approximation.

For property, travel, and product marketing, preserve factual accuracy: do not represent invented amenities, destinations, product geometry, or views as verified. Label concept renders and demo content. Use imagery that communicates the actual product. Missing high-quality media limits fidelity to the reference; communicate this when material.
